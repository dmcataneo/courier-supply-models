How it works

Hey! This one comes from a question I kept running into in delivery ops: do you book couriers into fixed shifts, or let anyone log in when they feel like it? Both work. They break in different places.

Pick a scene, move the knobs, and see where each model hurts. A rainy dinner, a slow Tuesday and a wrong forecast are a good start.

It's a small experiment on a made-up city, not a model of any real company or market. Every number is an input you can change.

Booked shifts. A planner books couriers for each hour from a forecast so they are about 70% busy. Booked couriers get a guaranteed hourly rate, whether orders show up or not. Some don't show up (no-shows). If the forecast is wrong, nobody can flex.

Open market. Couriers come online when their expected earnings per hour beat what they would accept (their reservation wage), and the platform pays per delivered order. The number online is the point where earnings and supply balance, and it moves only part of the way each hour (response speed). Great at flexing, less great at a sharp peak.

Hybrid. A booked base for the share of the plan you choose, with the open market topping up on top of it.

Orders and delivery time. A courier can deliver about 3 orders an hour when fully busy. The busier couriers are, the longer a delivery takes. Past about 40 minutes, customers start to give up, up to half of them. The curve is a simple rule of thumb, not measured data.
